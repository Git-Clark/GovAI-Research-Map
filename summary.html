In Summary

Allan Dafoe's research agenda argues that advanced AI is likely to become one of the most consequential forces of this century, and that the field studying how to govern it safely, called AI governance, is still dangerously underdeveloped relative to the stakes.

The paper organizes the open questions into three clusters that build on each other. The technical landscape asks what AI will actually be capable of, how fast that capability will arrive, and how hard it will be to keep it safe. AI politics asks how governments, companies, and the public will compete and cooperate as that capability spreads, and how it could reshape domestic power, the global economy, and international security. AI ideal governance steps back from what is likely to happen and asks what we should build if we could cooperate freely, what values should guide it, and what institutions could make that cooperation durable.

Dafoe's underlying point is that none of these questions can be answered by technologists alone. They require historians, political scientists, economists, and, notably for this class, journalists who can help the public understand what is at stake and hold both companies and governments accountable as the technology develops.
